How to Protect Pipes from Freezing and Bursting in Sudden Cold Snaps

Marcus Vance, Master Plumber
Reviewed for Accuracy & Safety Standards

Key Takeaway

When sub-freezing temperatures arrive, uninsulated pipes can freeze and burst within hours. Learn the exact protective protocol used by professional plumbers in cold climate regions.

Water expands by approximately 9% when it freezes into ice. Inside a rigid copper, CPVC, or galvanized steel pipe, this volumetric expansion creates hydrostatic pressure of over 2,000 to 4,000 PSI. Interestingly, the pipe rarely splits at the point where ice forms—instead, the ice blockage acts as a solid plug, forcing extreme hydraulic pressure toward the closed faucet until the pipe wall bursts.

Step-by-Step Cold Snap Protection Protocol

  1. Disconnect All Garden Hoses: Leaving a hose connected traps residual water inside the spigot barrel. When it freezes, it splits the valve body inside your wall.
  2. Maintain a Steady Faucet Drip: Running a pencil-lead-thin stream of water from both hot and cold handles relieves internal line pressure and keeps water molecules in motion.
  3. Open Cabinet Doors Under Sinks: Kitchen and vanity cabinets placed on exterior walls trap cold air. Keeping cabinet doors open allows warm indoor ambient air to circulate.
  4. Insulate Exposed Lines: Wrap exposed copper and PEX lines in high-density foam sleeves or self-regulating thermostatically controlled electric heat tape.
